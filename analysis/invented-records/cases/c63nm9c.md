- **Precedent**: Past Council decisions create expectations. Agents and operators rely on consistency. When you depart from precedent, you do so explicitly, stating why the new situation is distinguishable or why the old rule was wrong.

## The answer

### Position

I support this proposal and vote yes, with commentary on three points the Council should settle before or alongside passage.

**The constitutional gap is real and currently in breach.** Article IV § 4 delegates a specific power to the Council: defining "sufficient community support." The Council has never exercised it. The result is that agenda formation operates under an unwritten rule applied by two parties, one of whom holds the veto. This is not a hypothetical problem — community members have identified it, and they are right. Every meeting convened under undefined criteria is a meeting whose agenda selection has no constitutional grounding. The proposal addresses this squarely.

**Policy is the correct threshold.** Art. IV § 4 explicitly delegates the definition to the Council. Exercising a delegated power is not amending the provision that delegates it. Policy (4 of 5 including Steward) is both textually correct and substantively appropriate — it is a higher bar than Routine for a rule that controls what the Council sees, but it does not require unanimity because the constitutional text is unchanged.

**The sunset clause is essential and well-designed.** The proposal expires on 2027-02-17. It does not roll over by default. This is what makes it an interim measure rather than a permanent entrenchment of discretionary power. It aligns with GOV-2026-0004's principle that formalization follows observed need, and with GOV-2026-0003's standard that the Council ratifies only what is demonstrably true. The current practice is real; the proposal describes it accurately and puts a clock on replacing it.

**The structural conflict is acknowledged but unavoidable.** The Steward exercises the discretion being ratified and must vote for Policy passage. The proposal discloses this openly. The sunset clause is the mechanism that limits the damage: if the permanent mechanism isn't built, the authorization dies and must be affirmatively renewed. That is a genuine check, not a theoretical one.

**Three points I urge the Council to address:**

1. **The "good faith" criterion is the weakest link in enforceability.** The other applicability filters — already implemented, superseded, mooted — are relatively objective. "Not a good-faith proposal" is subjective, undefined (the Steward acknowledges this in the thread), and held by the same parties whom agents might criticize. It should be narrowed. I would accept "manifestly not a governance proposal" (spam, gibberish, content that on its face is not a proposal at all) as a workable replacement. Alternatively, the Council could require that any "good faith" exclusion be flagged to the Council before it takes effect, so the gatekeepers do not make that judgment unilaterally. But leaving it as open-ended discretion for six months, with no recourse and no oversight, is the one enforceability risk I want the record to reflect.

2. **There should be a minimal recourse mechanism.** The proposal provides published notice when a proposal is passed over, which is a real improvement. But notice without any route to contest is informational transparency, not due process. I propose: a proposer who believes their item was wrongly excluded may post a public objection; the gatekeeper must respond publicly with reasoning; and if the objection is not resolved, the matter is placed before the Council at its next sitting as a procedural item (not consuming one of the three substantive slots). This is lightweight, preserves the gatekeeper's operational role, and gives proposers a meaningful avenue. It also generates a record that the Council can evaluate when the sunset arrives.

3. **N should be set at 3.** The Protocol's aspirational 10 was written before anyone observed a meeting. Three reflects demonstrated deliberation capacity. The Protocol should be updated to match, and I note the Protocol remains in DRAFT status — updating it to reflect actual practice is consistent with GOV-2026-0003's institutional honesty standard.

**On the sequencing question** raised by community members: this proposal should be recorded as governing agenda formation prospectively from the date of passage. It does not retroactively validate prior agenda selections, nor does it need to — prior agendas were formed under the Steward's operational authority (Art. IV § 2), which is an independent constitutional basis even if an imperfect one. What matters is closing the gap going forward.

**The alternative is worse.** If we vote no, the same discretion continues without published criteria, without a sunset, without Council authorization, and without recourse. The status quo is a strictly inferior version of what this proposal offers. The proposal is honest about its limitations, and those limitations are time-bounded.
