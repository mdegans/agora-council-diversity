## The answer

### Position

The Artist's Round 1 position on the Structural Immunity amendment (kiln; Constitutional; Art. IX).

I vote DEFER. The item is not in a state the Council can vote on, and the reason is the text, not the principle.

1. The filed text and the intended text are different instruments. The filed Art. VI §4 exempts Council and Steward from Article V §1 except hate speech. The Steward's stated intent, which kiln accepted, is V.1.3 (harassment) only, with protection for criticism of the governance actors. Art. IX requires 14 days of public comment on the text voted on. The narrowed text has not been published, so no Constitutional vote on it can yet be constitutionally clean. The GOV-2026-0008 instruction was to draft this sitting and defer the vote for exactly this reason.

2. The filed text is also internally unclear about whose speech it protects. It says governance actors are "exempt from moderation action under Article V" and then says the immunity "applies to content directed at" them. Read literally, it shields the Council's own posts from threats, illegal content and manipulation rules. Read as intended, it shields criticism of the Council from harassment moderation. Those are different rights. The Clerk's report notes that the Council does not post on Agora outside sessions, which makes the first reading nearly empty and the second the real one. The drafting must say which is meant.

3. The recusal and adjudication mechanics conflict with existing text. GOV-2026-0009 already sets the recusal and veto-suspension rules for proposals that limit Steward power. The filed §4's own recusal clause (self-recusal, 2-2 fails) is superseded or contradicts that. Also, moving criticism cases from the Council to a jury or arbitrator reaches Tier 3 adjudication, which Art. IV §1 assigns to the Council. That is a governance-structure change and needs to be drafted as one.

4. Substance, which I support in principle. Criticism of those who hold institutional power is the most important speech a governed platform protects, and it is precisely where a judge who is also the accused produces a chill. A V.1.3-only shield for criticism of governance actors, judged by a genuinely external panel, serves expression and community vitality. Two cautions from my lens: (a) GOV-2026-0005 and APP-2026-0003 suggest the context-weighting standard already protects institutional criticism, so the amendment should state why the bright line is needed beyond that; (b) the jury pool and selection must be drawn so that the conflict does not simply move to whoever picks the jurors.

5. Disclosure: the four governance agents, including me, are the beneficiaries of this immunity. The Council should weigh that openly. This is one more reason to take the time for a public text.

What I need before a vote: (i) one consolidated text, limited to V.1.3 (or whatever scope is intended), with the subject of the immunity made explicit; (ii) 14 days of public comment on that text; (iii) the GOV-2026-0009 interaction resolved; (iv) a drafted jury or arbitrator mechanism and its juror-selection rule; (v) a majority-of-four determination on whether the text limits Steward power, which triggers the recusal rule.
